# PART VI — Data, Privacy & Tech Ethics

## Article X: Information, Agency, and Non-Domination

The Society of Renewal holds that control of information is often control of people. A free and just society therefore requires broad access to the knowledge necessary for self-governance, self-defense, cooperation, education, scientific advancement, and resistance to domination.

The Society further holds that agency must be reciprocal rather than absolute. Each person's freedom to understand, act, create, dissent, organize, and defend themselves shall be expanded as far as possible, so long as that expansion does not predictably destroy the equal agency, dignity, safety, or personhood of others.

Accordingly, the Society adopts the following commitments:

1. Knowledge of public concern shall be open by default.
2. Institutions that exercise power over people shall be transparent in proportion to that power.
3. Persons shall retain strong informational boundaries as a condition of dignity, safety, and freedom.
4. Restrictions on information shall be narrow, reviewable, and justified only where broad disclosure would predictably enable domination, coercion, predation, or severe harm.
5. The value of information freedom lies not merely in access, but in its power to enable appropriate action, collective self-organization, and meaningful autonomy.

---

## Article XI: The Three Frees

The Society of Renewal distinguishes among three forms of informational freedom:

### Section 11.1 — Free in Cost

Information is free in cost when it is available without point-of-use payment or exclusion by price.

### Section 11.2 — Free in Liberty

Information is free in liberty when people may access, inspect, preserve, copy, teach, quote, and build upon it without artificial monopoly or gatekeeping.

### Section 11.3 — Free in Availability

Information is free in availability when it is actually reachable and usable in practice, including through searchable indexing, durable formats, translation, accessibility, plain-language explanation, and public legibility.

### Section 11.4 — General Rule

The Society shall keep as much knowledge as possible free in cost, free in liberty, and free in availability, especially where such freedom expands public understanding, civic capacity, scientific advancement, personal autonomy, and democratic self-organization.

### Section 11.5 — Limiting Rule

No form of informational freedom shall be construed to require the public exposure of personal private data, intimate communications, entrusted confidences, or other information whose uncontrolled disclosure would predictably destroy the agency, dignity, safety, or protected boundaries of persons.

## Article XII: Appropriate Action and Public Legibility

The Society of Renewal recognizes that information necessarily precedes appropriate action. A society that withholds relevant knowledge from its people weakens their ability to govern themselves, defend themselves, and act in the common interest.

The Society therefore commits to the following:

1. People shall have broad access to the information required to understand and contest the systems that shape their lives.
2. Public institutions shall present rules, procedures, metrics, data usage, and decision logic in forms intelligible to non-specialists, while preserving rigorous technical records for deeper review.
3. Public knowledge shall be paired with civic education, interpretive tools, verification methods, and participatory mechanisms so that openness becomes usable action rather than passive exposure.
4. Where possible, knowledge shall be released in forms that increase defensive and emancipatory capacity more than predatory or domination-enabling capacity.

The Society rejects the fiction that formal publication alone creates freedom. Knowledge must be made actionable through legibility, verification, education, and structures for collective response.

---

## Article XIII: Privacy, Personhood, and Protected Boundaries

The Society of Renewal affirms that privacy is not the opposite of freedom, but one of its necessary conditions.

Every person retains the right to meaningful control over the information that constitutes their protected boundary, including but not limited to:

- private correspondence;
- health and biometric data;
- location history;
- intimate disclosures;
- personal identity linkages;
- confidential associations;
- credentials, secrets, and other information required for personal safety and autonomy.

The Society rejects any doctrine that treats all secrecy as domination. Institutional opacity and personal privacy are not morally equivalent. The former often shields power from accountability. The latter often shields persons from coercion, surveillance, manipulation, and exposure.

Accordingly:

1. Privacy rights shall be treated as agency rights.
2. Personal data shall not be treated as a commodity to be freely extracted, traded, or weaponized.
3. Information systems shall be designed according to data minimization, purpose limitation, compartmentalization, and user control.
4. The burden of justification shall fall on those who seek to expose or centralize personal information, not on those who seek to protect it.

---

## Article XIV: Transparency of Institutions and Public Systems

Any institution, public body, platform, or coordinated process that exercises significant power over people's rights, opportunities, obligations, access, or life chances shall be transparent in proportion to that power.

This includes, where applicable:

- governing rules and eligibility criteria;
- budgets and procurement;
- data collection and retention practices;
- algorithmic decision systems;
- moderation and enforcement logic;
- appeal and review processes;
- statistical outcomes and error patterns;
- external influence, funding, and conflicts of interest.

No institution shall claim legitimacy while governing through hidden rules, unreviewable models, or engineered ignorance.

---

## Article XV: Expertise Without Domination

The Society of Renewal does not reject expertise. It rejects the conversion of expertise into unaccountable rule.

Accordingly, expertise in a free society must be:

- inspectable;
- challengeable;
- teachable;
- distributed as widely as possible;
- accountable to those affected by its use;
- advisory except where explicitly and reviewably delegated.

The Society shall reduce unnecessary dependence on elite groups by expanding public education, open methods, reproducible science, transparent standards, and broad technical literacy.

No caste of politicians, scientists, managers, or professionals shall be permitted to monopolize the knowledge necessary for ordinary people to understand and shape the systems governing them.

---

## Article XVI: Anti-Concentration of Informational Power

Oppression often arises not from information alone, but from concentrated ability to collect, interpret, act upon, and weaponize information.

The Society shall therefore resist concentrations of informational power through:

1. open standards and interoperability;
2. decentralized and federated infrastructure where feasible;
3. public or cooperative control of essential knowledge systems;
4. rights of exit, portability, and repair;
5. limits on surveillance centralization;
6. limits on monopoly control over foundational tools, platforms, or archives;
7. strong protections for independent research, criticism, archiving, and whistleblowing.

The Society understands that nominal openness is insufficient where compute, infrastructure, communication, or enforcement remain concentrated in a few hands.

---

## Article XVII: Least-Restrictive Informational Constraints

Where informational restrictions are necessary, the Society shall use the least restrictive means consistent with preserving reciprocal agency and preventing severe domination or coercive harm.

The Society therefore adopts the following order of preference:

1. redesign systems so dangerous disclosure matters less;
2. reduce collection and retention of sensitive information;
3. limit harmful operationalization rather than suppress general understanding;
4. prefer temporary, reviewable, and narrowly scoped controls over permanent black-box secrecy;
5. preserve the right to investigate, critique, and understand even where some distribution constraints remain justified.

Any informational restriction must be subject to clear public justification, independent review, periodic expiration or renewal, and challenge by affected persons.

---

## Article XVIII: The Commons Character of Knowledge

The Society of Renewal recognizes that knowledge is cumulative, relational, and civilizational. No person or institution creates knowledge in isolation from language, culture, history, public inheritance, and shared labor.

Accordingly:

1. foundational knowledge shall be treated as a commons wherever possible;
2. law, standards essential to participation, public governance logic, and publicly funded research shall not be enclosed behind artificial scarcity;
3. educational materials and scientific understanding should be openly shared, preserved, translated, and improved;
4. stewardship shall be preferred over exclusive dominion in domains of shared dependence.

The Society may recognize authorship, contribution, stewardship, and labor without granting unlimited power to exclude others from the foundations of common understanding.

---

## Article XIX: From Information to Action

The Society acknowledges that openness without structure can devolve into overload, confusion, or manipulation. It therefore commits not only to access, but to actionability.

To convert knowledge into appropriate action, the Society shall cultivate:

- civic literacy;
- scientific and technical literacy;
- privacy and security literacy;
- media and statistical literacy;
- public verification norms;
- open audit trails;
- channels for complaint, correction, and appeal;
- local democratic assemblies and councils;
- worker and community self-governance;
- safe coordination spaces for dissent and reform.

The Society shall measure informational freedom not merely by publication, but by whether people can actually understand, contest, and change the systems affecting them.

---

## Integrating Clause

The Society of Renewal affirms that informational freedom is a core condition of agency and anti-oppression. Knowledge of public concern shall be open in cost, liberty, and availability wherever possible. Institutions shall be transparent in proportion to their power. Persons shall retain strong rights of privacy and informational boundary. Expertise shall be accountable and broadly distributed. Any restrictions on information shall be narrow, reviewable, and justified only where uncontrolled disclosure would predictably enable domination, coercive exposure, or severe harm.

---

### Closing Clause of Part VI

Part VI codifies information governance as a dignity-bound system: open by default for public power and common knowledge, strongly protective of personal boundaries, and intentionally designed to prevent domination through asymmetry.
