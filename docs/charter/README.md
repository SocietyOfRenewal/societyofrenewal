The Charter of Renewal is the Society's draft constitutional framework.

The Charter derives from the Founding Book:

- Part A states the moral covenant and the Dignity Constraint.
- Part B develops the evidence, hypotheses, and architecture from which rights, institutions, and protocols are designed.
- The Charter states the powers and commitments the Society intends to make binding.
- Essentia and other systems implement those commitments and return evidence to the book.

The Charter is not ratified law, and no currency, benefit, civic identity, binding election, or Society-operated safety system is live.

## Founding monetary hypothesis

Part B gives the Charter an explicit monetary direction:

> A cryptographically accountable network can create a positive-value, broadly accepted currency through shared agreement, equal civic issuance, useful exchange, standing acceptance commitments, dense trade loops, productive credit, transparent rules, and a stable real unit of account. If that equilibrium becomes durable, an Essential Unit can convert through an Essential Settlement Receivable into enough Essent to provide a real and usable universal income.

The intended flow is:

```text
𝒰 -> ℛ -> ℰ
```

- `𝒰` measures the indexed real entitlement.
- `ℛ` preserves the activated claim during settlement.
- `ℰ` is the common money, created through both outside civic issuance and inside credit.
- The External Liquidity Bridge connects the internal economy to national currencies, imports, and outside institutions without defining all `ℰ` as a reserve-backed claim.

The Charter does not presume that every monetary unit requires a conventional debtor. It requires transparent issuance, equal standing, measurable purchasing power, productive capacity, provider participation, public monetary governance, and a visible response when prices, shortages, confidence, or acceptance move in the wrong direction.

## Current parts

### Part I - Research Basis and Constitutional Implications

Defines the Society's social and monetary hypotheses, bounded evidence conclusions, constitutional constraints, `𝒰 -> ℛ -> ℰ`, and the relationship between invention, testing, dependency, and ratification.

### Part II - Operational Articles

Defines plural purpose-bound identity, public and private Essentia planes, governance by decision type, and the constitutional core of `𝒰 -> ℛ -> ℰ`. It treats direct indexed conversion, equal outside civic issuance, productive capacity, provider reuse, and the External Liquidity Bridge as one connected monetary design.

### Part III - Safeguards, Restoration & Peacekeeping

Proposes prevention, de-escalation, restorative options, protective intervention, review, and defense. Its institutions, tools, and timelines require professional, legal, medical, human-rights, and empirical development.

### Part IV - Open Participation, Membership, and Federation

Separates open participation from high-assurance eligibility, defines plural and portable credentials, and establishes node federation around explicit monetary exposure, cross-node clearing, lawful operation, sanctuary, continuity, and exit.

### Part VI - Data, Privacy & Tech Ethics

States information-freedom, privacy, transparency, and anti-domination commitments. Its mechanisms must remain compatible with purpose limitation, plural identity, recovery, accessibility, and the separation between public institutional evidence and private human life.

## Development rule

A constitutional value may precede the final mechanism. Dignity does not need to wait for a randomized trial.

A novel mechanism may also be developed before full-scale evidence exists. The purpose of the research program is to create that evidence.

As a mechanism becomes harder to reverse and more necessary to daily life, the Society must require stronger modeling, operational safeguards, independent evaluation, continuity, appeal, and correction. This protects people without granting inherited institutions a monopoly on what counts as possible.
