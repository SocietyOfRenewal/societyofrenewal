## Current state

The Society is still in formation. The phases below describe possible directions,
not live services or committed launch dates.

Today:

- the Manifesto is published;
- Part A and Part B of the Founding Book are complete working drafts;
- the Charter is a draft derived from Part B and has not been ratified;
- Essentia v0.1.0 is runnable research software that does not implement the current architecture;
- Essentia v0.8.0 is the current research architecture, not an implemented network;
- the Academy is an early beta;
- and no Freedom Floor, currency, civic identity, binding voting, or benefit system is live.

## Proposed program roadmap

### Phase I - Public foundations and research infrastructure

The first phase is the work already underway: publish the Society's principles,
make their assumptions inspectable, test narrow implementations, and build the
measurement, accounting, privacy, governance, security, and verification work
required before any integrated civic platform could responsibly operate.

Current priorities are:

- **Founding Book:** maintain the distinction between moral commitments, empirical evidence, design conjectures, and operational claims.
- **Charter development:** derive identity, governance, safety, and economy articles from Part B, then test them before ratification.
- **Open evidence:** publish decision registers, sources, assumptions, pilot protocols, failure criteria, and negative results.
- **Academy:** test accessible learning and guidance without treating completion metrics as proof of social impact.
- **Essentia simulation:** model essential baskets, 𝒰 claims, provider settlement, mutual credit, reserves, market conversion, defaults, and stress modes before building a new consensus network.

### Phase II - Local capacity and shadow clearing

Before issuing money or benefits, cooperating local organizations would map real
capacity and run accounting in parallel with ordinary payment systems.

Possible work includes:

- local essential-basket research using price data and resident deliberation;
- provider capacity contracts and procurement planning;
- shadow Essential Settlement Receivables;
- obligation-netting and mutual-credit simulations;
- reserve and external-liquidity planning;
- privacy-preserving eligibility and recovery trials;
- local deliberative assemblies and decision-method experiments;
- and independent legal, security, economic, accessibility, and human-rights review.

No participant should depend on a shadow ledger balance for survival.

### Phase III - Bounded pilots

Only after the accounting and institutional layers survive earlier gates should
the Society test small real-value systems.

Possible pilots include:

- funded 𝒰 entitlements for a narrow population and basket;
- real provider settlement under published coverage thresholds;
- mutual-credit ℰ among a dense trade loop with explicit limits and loss allocation;
- bounded ℰ conversion through auctions or reserve-backed corridors;
- small unrestricted dividends funded by actual revenue or surplus;
- and public evaluation against precommitted success and stop conditions.

A pilot is not evidence of global viability. Expansion requires replication under
different legal, economic, cultural, and supply conditions.

### Phase IV - Federation

Nodes that have demonstrated reliable accounting, settlement, privacy, governance,
and correction may federate.

Federation requires:

- mutual exposure limits;
- issuer and node identifiers on liabilities;
- reciprocal acceptance agreements;
- cross-node clearing and settlement;
- creditor and debtor adjustment;
- transparent reserves and coverage;
- exit and resolution rules;
- and no assumption that every ℰ balance carries identical risk merely because it shares a symbol.

### Phase V - Distributed public infrastructure

Only after governance and economic federation work should the Society consider a
shared Byzantine-fault-tolerant ledger or broader mesh infrastructure.

Possible directions include:

- signed append-only public records with independent witnesses;
- distributed education, civic records, and ecological data;
- plural, purpose-bound credentials rather than one universal identity;
- machine-assisted explanation and summarization with auditable human authority;
- cryptographic agility and post-quantum migration;
- and resilient local operation without a single technical or institutional point of failure.

The network architecture follows the trust model. The trust model does not follow
the desire to have a blockchain.

## Working commitments

The project begins from commitments that remain open to evidence, criticism, and
revision in their implementation:

1. **Dignity constrains power.** No person or conscious being becomes disposable because of status, capacity, conformity, or usefulness.
2. **Interdependence is unavoidable.** The practical question is whether it is organized through reciprocity and care or through dependence and domination.
3. **Stabilization, education, and repair should precede coercion when they can safely do so.** Some immediate threats still require the least restrictive effective intervention.
4. **Economic promises must attach to real capacity.** A balance, peg, or conversion formula is not a Freedom Floor unless people can obtain the promised essentials.
5. **Progress must be observable.** Reduced suffering, expanded agency, ecological repair, accessible knowledge, and accountable power matter more than ideological purity or institutional self-preservation.

This is a proposal for an ecosystem of understanding and participation. It is
not presently a government, public authority, benefit program, currency issuer,
or operating monetary system.
